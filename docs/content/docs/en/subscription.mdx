## Adding a subscription

On the **Subscriptions** tab tap **Add subscription** (once you have subscriptions — **+** in the
header), paste the link and tap "Add subscription". `https://…` links work, and so do
`clash://install-config?url=…` and `clodclash://install-config?url=…` — those can be opened
straight from a browser or the provider's portal.

The first subscription becomes active right away. You can have several; to switch, tap the card
of the one you want. The active subscription's name is shown in the home screen header.

**Other ways to import** — load a config from a file or from an arbitrary URL.

### Secure connection

It is not plain HTTPS (that is always used anyway) but an extra layer of encryption on top of
it: the link, device details and config travel inside it, hidden from anyone on the way. It
works when the provider has the channel.

There is no switch on the adding screen: the app tries the secure channel first and shows
what is going on — "Checking the provider's secure channel…".

- The channel is there — the subscription is added over it.
- The provider has no secure channel — the subscription is loaded the usual way, and the app
  says it was added without the secure channel.
- The server does not answer — the app tries the secure channel again, three attempts in all,
  then says "The provider's server is not responding"; the subscription is not added.

In the subscription properties (card menu **⋮** → **Edit**) the channel can be turned off —
with a warning that this is not safe — and on again: turning it on is checked when you save,
and if the channel does not answer, it stays off.

A subscription with the secure channel sends the provider a **server quality report** after a
scheduled refresh, and between refreshes if they are less frequent than every 6 hours (at most
once every 6 hours): latency and "16–20" check results per server, traffic through them, the
kind of network (Wi-Fi, mobile, wired) and the external address the measurements were made from
(the app learns it from Yandex, outside the tunnel). The network name, location, sites and apps
are not included. The report goes only over the secure channel and only if the provider has
turned on receiving it: once the provider says it does not accept reports, the app stops
collecting and deletes the collected measurements.

## Updating

The subscription updates by itself if the provider sets an interval. Your own interval is in the
card menu (**⋮**) → **Edit** → **Auto update** — at least 15 minutes; **Use the panel value**
brings back the provider's one. With no interval from either side, the subscription only updates
manually.

Manually: the update icon in the home screen header, **Update** in the card menu, or
**Update all** in the tab header. When the subscription's expiry from the panel comes, the app
updates it once more by itself, so a renewal or the "expired" notice shows up right away.

**Subscription update errors** and **Successful subscription updates** notifications are on by
default and can be turned off in **More → Look and behavior**.

## Traffic and expiry

While the tunnel is off, the home screen shows the "Traffic" and "Expires in" cards — if the
provider sends these numbers. When three days or fewer are left, traffic has run out or the
subscription has expired, a subscription card with "Customer portal" and "Support" buttons takes
their place.

<Shot src="en/home.png" alt="Traffic and expiry cards on the home screen" />

**Subscription reminders** (also in "Look and behavior") warn you in advance about expiry and
traffic; the provider sets the thresholds, otherwise 7, 3 and 1 days before and at 80, 90 and
100 % of traffic.

## Moving to another device

**More → Look and behavior → Back up subscriptions** saves your link subscriptions to a file
together with names and intervals. Keep the file private: the links give access to your
subscription. On the new device use **Restore subscriptions**; links already added are skipped.
