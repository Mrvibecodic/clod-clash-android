## Where to get the link

Your VPN service gives you the subscription link — usually in the customer portal, a Telegram bot
or an email after payment. It looks like this:

```
https://sub.example.com/a1b2c3d4
```

Only `https://` links are accepted. Invite links `clodclash://install-config?url=…` and
`clash://install-config?url=…` can be opened from the browser or the provider's portal: the app opens
the new subscription's properties with the link filled in — tap the save button in the header. You
can also paste such a link (or
`clashmeta://install-config?url=…`) into the field instead of a regular one.

<Callout type="warn" title="The link is a key">
Anyone with the link can use your subscription. Do not publish it or forward it to strangers.
</Callout>

## Add a subscription

<Steps>
<Step>

Open the **Subscriptions** tab and tap **Add subscription**. If you already have subscriptions, the
button is **+** in the tab header.

</Step>
<Step>

Paste the link into **Subscription link** and tap **Add subscription**.

<Shot src="ru/add-profile.png" alt="Add screen: the link field and the Add subscription button" />

</Step>
<Step>

Wait a few seconds. First the app checks whether the provider has a secure channel, then it
downloads the subscription and checks it. The screen tells you what is going on.

<Shot src="ru/add-profile-checking.png" alt="Loading: “Checking the provider's secure channel…”" />

</Step>
<Step>

Done: the app returns to the home screen and says “Subscription “…” added”. The first subscription
becomes the selected one right away — you can connect.

</Step>
</Steps>

If the subscription cannot be added, the reason appears under the link field and nothing is saved.
What each reason means — in [Common errors](/en/docs/user/errors#a-subscription-cannot-be-added).

<Shot src="ru/add-profile-error.png" alt="An error under the field: the provider did not find the subscription (code 404)" />

**Other ways to import** (the button under the field) — a subscription from a configuration file on
the phone or from an arbitrary URL. Regular users do not need them.

### Secure connection

This is not plain HTTPS (that is always used) but an extra layer of encryption with the provider on
top of it: the link, the device details and the server list travel inside it, and middlemen on the
way cannot see them. It works only if the provider supports the channel.

There is no switch when adding — the app decides by itself:

- the provider has the channel — the subscription is added through it;
- there is no channel — the app says “The provider has no secure channel — loading the usual way…”
  and adds the subscription the regular way;
- the server does not answer — the app tries again (“No answer, trying again (2 of 3)…”), three
  attempts in total, then says “The provider's server is not responding”; nothing is added.

## Several subscriptions

You can have as many subscriptions as you like, but only one works — the selected one: its card is
highlighted and its name is shown in the home screen header. To switch, tap another card. The label
on the right of a card is the state of the subscription itself: **Active** (in force), **Expiring**
(three days or less left), **Out of traffic**, **Expired**.

<Shot src="ru/subscriptions.png" alt="Subscriptions tab: the selected subscription, another one, and one that has not updated for a while" />

A card shows used traffic and days left. The card menu **⋮**:

| Item | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Update** | download the subscription again right now |
| **Edit** | open the properties: name, link, auto update, secure connection |
| **Group** | sort subscriptions into groups — tabs “All” and the group names appear above the list |
| **Delete** | erase the subscription and its settings; cannot be undone |

## Subscription properties

Card menu **⋮ → Edit**. Save the changes with the button in the header.

<Shot src="ru/properties.png" alt="Subscription properties: name, link, auto update, secure connection" />

- **Name** — your own name for the subscription. Empty — the provider's name is shown. If you set
  your own, the provider's name stays next to it in brackets.
- **URL** — the subscription link. If the provider gave you a new link, paste it here: the selected
  servers, mode and favorites are kept.
- **Auto update** — how often the subscription is downloaded again, in minutes, at least 15. Until
  you set your own value, the provider sends the interval (“Set by the subscription panel”).
  **Use the panel value** returns the provider's interval. An empty field with no interval from the
  provider means manual updates only.
- **Secure connection** — can be turned off if your provider asks for it: the app warns that this
  is unsafe. You can turn it back on right there — the channel is checked on save, and if it does
  not answer, it stays off. Below is the provider key fingerprint.
- **Browse files** — the downloaded configuration and provider files. Only for those who know what
  is in there.

## Updating the subscription

The subscription updates by itself at the interval from its properties. By hand:

- the update icon in the home screen header — for the selected subscription;
- **⋮ → Update** on the card;
- **Update all** in the Subscriptions tab header.

When the expiry date comes, the app updates the subscription once more by itself — so a renewal or
the end message shows up at once, even with auto update off.

If an auto update fails, the card shows “Not updated · updated …”. If it fails twice in a row, the
card gets a frame saying **Update the subscription**, and the update icon on the home screen is
highlighted. Tap **Update**: if the error repeats, the app shows the reason. A successful update
clears the marks.

The **Subscription update errors** and **Successful subscription updates** notifications are on by
default and turned off in **More → Look and behavior**.

## Traffic and expiry

While the tunnel is off and the subscription is Active, the home screen shows the **Traffic** and
**Expires in** cards if the provider sends these numbers. When three days or less are left, the
traffic has run out or the subscription has expired, a subscription card replaces them above the connect button with the
**Customer portal** and **Support** buttons (if the provider sent these links).

**Subscription reminders** (in **More → Look and behavior**) warn in advance about the end of the
term and traffic. The thresholds come from the provider; without them — 7, 3 and 1 day before, and
at 80, 90 and 100 % of traffic.

## Moving to another device

**More → Look and behavior → Back up subscriptions** writes the links of all subscriptions with
their names and intervals to a file. On the new phone — **Restore subscriptions** and pick that
file: the app shows how many subscriptions are in the file and which one becomes selected. Links
that are already added are not duplicated.

<Callout type="warn" title="Do not share the backup file">
The file holds the subscription links, and they give access to your VPN.
</Callout>

If your provider limits the number of devices, the new phone takes another slot. Only the provider
can free a slot — contact their support.
